## All distinct objections (31)
**01. [Slot 1] Migration cutover creates deployment freeze**

> §7.6.2 blocks ALL products from using the new ≥95 bar until 100% of legacy ClearanceRecords are migrated. This creates a system-wide deployment freeze where no product can ship at the new standard until every legacy product is addressed, potentially blocking critical updates for weeks.

**02. [Slot 1] Admin bottleneck for routine operations**

> §7.6.1 requires admin role for ANY sub-95 deployment, even temporary staging deployments or emergency hotfixes. The 80-char rationale requirement and governance_record audit trail create friction that could delay critical fixes when the single admin is unavailable.

**03. [Slot 1] Expiry cliff creates operational chaos**

> §7.6.1 hard-expires overrides at 14 days OR next pipeline run, whichever is first. A product at 94 score suddenly fails Step 5 mid-deployment cycle with no warning, requiring emergency admin re-authorization even if nothing changed about the product's actual quality.

**04. [Slot 1] No escape hatch for systemic scoring bugs**

> If the §7.6 scoring engine has a bug that artificially deflates scores across all products, the admin must issue individual expiring overrides for EVERY affected product every 14 days. There's no provision for a temporary system-wide scoring adjustment during bug fixes.

**05. [Slot 2] Admin-only override risks abuse**

> The admin-only expiring override mechanism in §7.6.1 could be abused by admins to bypass the ≥95 GTM bar frequently, undermining the integrity of the standard. Without strict oversight or additional checks, this could lead to a culture of complacency where the override becomes the norm rather than the exception.

**06. [Slot 2] Migration path complexity**

> The ClearanceRecord migration path outlined in §7.6.2 is complex and may introduce significant operational overhead. The requirement for re-evaluation and potential re-categorization of existing records could lead to delays and errors, especially if the migration script encounters unforeseen issues.

**07. [Slot 2] Lack of operator involvement**

> By removing the operator-configurable knob and making the override admin-only, the proposal excludes operators from the decision-making process regarding GTM readiness. This could lead to frustration and disengagement from operators who feel they have valuable insights into product readiness.

**08. [Slot 3] Lack of Rollback**

> The proposal does not provide a clear rollback plan in case the new ≥95 canonical bar causes unintended consequences, such as a significant decrease in product deployments. This could lead to a situation where the system is worse than the status quo, as operators may struggle to meet the new bar.

**09. [Slot 3] Insufficient Testing**

> The proposal does not specify how the new admin-only expiring override mechanism will be tested to ensure it works correctly and does not introduce new bugs or security vulnerabilities. This lack of testing could lead to issues down the line, such as overrides not expiring correctly or being used in unintended ways.

**10. [Slot 3] Potential for Abuse**

> The admin-only expiring override mechanism could be abused by admins to consistently deploy products with low scores, undermining the purpose of the ≥95 canonical bar. There is no clear mechanism in place to prevent or detect such abuse, which could lead to a degradation of the system's overall quality.

**11. [Slot 4] Override Mechanism Complexity**

> The admin-only expiring override mechanism in §7.6.1 introduces significant complexity. The multiple rules for expiry and use, along with the need for detailed audit trails, could lead to implementation errors and increased maintenance burden.

**12. [Slot 4] Migration Path Risk**

> The migration path for ClearanceRecord entities in §7.6.2 requires a complete inventory and re-evaluation of all existing entities. This process is time-consuming and risky, as any errors in re-evaluation could lead to incorrect status assignments and potential compliance issues.

**13. [Slot 4] Conflation Invariant Redundancy**

> The mandatory invariant in §19.0 that states 'These are NEVER conflated in any operator-facing surface' is redundant with existing governance mechanisms in §10. This redundancy adds unnecessary complexity and could lead to confusion among operators.

**14. [Slot 5] Migration Complexity**

> The migration path for ClearanceRecord entities is complex and may introduce errors or delays in the system.

**15. [Slot 5] Admin Override Abuse**

> The admin-only expiring override mechanism could be abused by admins to bypass the ≥95 bar without proper justification.

**16. [Slot 5] Near-GTM Band Elimination**

> Eliminating the Near-GTM 90–94 conditional band without a fallback clause may be too rigid and could lead to unnecessary delays in product deployment.

**17. [Slot 6] Migration cutover deadlock risk**

> §7.6.2 cutover gate requires 100% of pending ClearanceRecord entities to resolve to (a)/(b)/(c) before the ≥95 bar activates. But there is no mechanism preventing NEW ClearanceRecord entities from being created during the migration window — a product created mid-migration would be tagged `migration_pending` and could block cutover indefinitely if the engineering team cannot resolve all legacy entities. The cutover envelope `ca13v3_cutover_complete.v1` may never fire, leaving the system permanently on the old 75-bar standard.

**18. [Slot 6] Override renewal loop — permanent sub-95 deployment**

> §7.6.1 grants admin authority to issue a fresh override after expiry with no cooldown, no spending limit, and no escalation requirement. An admin can issue a 30-day override for a product at score 75, then immediately issue another 30-day override when the first expires, in perpetuity. The audit trail records each grant individually but nowhere does the spec cap the number of sequential overrides per product. This transforms the 'admin-only expiring override' into a de facto permanent escape hatch — the exact structural problem the CEO decision was meant to eliminate.

**19. [Slot 6] Rationale quality gate is unenforceable**

> §7.6.1 requires `rationale: string ≥ 80 chars; cannot match canonical stop-words set` but the stop-words list is not defined in the proposal, no validation logic is specified, and there is no human-review gate on rationale quality. An admin can write 'This is necessary for business reasons to proceed with deployment' (80+ chars, no obvious stop-words) and pass the envelope write. The spec provides no automated quality check and no escalation trigger for low-quality rationales, making the ≥80-char requirement a pure formality.

**20. [Slot 6] Override floor rejection creates operational cliff**

> CA13A-v3-CT-3 specifies that `override_floor: 50` is REJECTED (not clamped). If an admin misconfigures the floor value and the override is rejected, the product falls immediately to the canonical ≥95 bar with no grace period. Unlike the migration path (which has a structured three-outcome resolution), there is no fallback mechanism for a rejected override envelope — the product simply fails Step 5 with no admin recourse documented in the spec. This creates a hard operational cliff where a single typo in an admin-issued override can halt deployment entirely.

**21. [Slot 6] Migration outcome (c) creates legacy underclass incentive**

> §7.6.2 outcome (c) allows admin annotation of ClearanceRecord as `legacy_pre_ca13v3` — these entities do not count toward any new §7.6 metrics. This creates a perverse incentive: any product that cannot score ≥95 can be re-categorized as legacy, removing it from the metric denominator entirely. The spec does not define who can apply outcome (c), what threshold triggers it, or whether there is a cap on legacy-archived entities. A product that scores 76 can be archived as legacy indefinitely, defeating the purpose of the ≥95 bar.

**22. [Slot 7] Migration script failure risk**

> §7.6.2 migration path lacks rollback mechanisms for failed re-evaluations. If the migration script encounters corrupted ClearanceRecord data during inventory pass, it could cascade failures across multiple products without recovery options. The conformance test CA13A-v3-CT-6 doesn't cover partial-failure states.

**23. [Slot 7] Override expiry race condition**

> §7.6.1 expiry rule 1 (next pipeline run) creates deployment race conditions. If two pipeline runs trigger simultaneously for the same product, override expiration timing becomes unpredictable. Audit envelope emission lacks atomicity guarantees with pipeline execution states.

**24. [Slot 7] Guard() cross-reference gap**

> B-Q1 EXECUTOR_REGISTRY cross-reference doesn't resolve Slot 10 #34's concern about BaseAgent.guard() argument validation. The spec doesn't mandate how authorityNeeded sets are populated for Agent #26's dual-authority requirement per CA-9-Q4(b).

**25. [Slot 7] Legacy categorization loophole**

> §7.6.2 outcome (c) lets admins bypass migration via legacy_archived status without score verification. This creates an audit trail gap where pre-CA13v3 products avoid scrutiny indefinitely under the new standard.

**26. [Slot 8] Override loophole persists**

> In §7.6.1 the proposal replaces the operator-config knob with an admin-only expiring override, but it still preserves a discretionary sub-95 escape hatch. Because admins can re-issue overrides repeatedly, the effective threshold can remain below 95 indefinitely, just with more ceremony and audit logging.

**27. [Slot 8] Migration cutover is under-specified**

> §7.6.2 says the ≥95 bar takes effect only after 100% of legacy ClearanceRecords resolve, but it does not specify a bounded enforcement path for stuck or abandoned records. A single unresolved or disputed legacy row could block cutover forever, or force ad hoc legacy-archiving decisions that weaken the standard.

**28. [Slot 8] Dual-bar UI confusion risk**

> §19.0 adds a mandatory anti-conflation invariant and surface-labeling, yet the artifact still allows both the Self-Audit 95/95 threshold and the GTM bar to coexist in operator-facing surfaces. Without a single authoritative display contract, operators may misread one score as satisfying the other, especially during migration when old and new ClearanceRecords coexist.

**29. [Slot 10] Migration cutover stalls entire bar**

> §7.6.2 requires 100% ClearanceRecord resolution (a/b/c outcomes) before emitting ca13v3_cutover_complete.v1 and activating the ≥95 bar. No timeout, partial-cutover path, or fallback for products lacking recent pipeline runs is defined, so a single stuck legacy record freezes the canonical threshold indefinitely.

**30. [Slot 10] Expiring override window too wide**

> §7.6.1 allows admin to set expires_at up to granted_at+30 days and ties one expiry trigger to 'next pipeline run,' which may be weeks away. This creates a controllable multi-week sub-95 deployment window with only governance_record_entry audit, weaker than the v2 operator knob it replaces.

**31. [Slot 10] Conformance tests lack enforcement spec**

> CA13A-v3-CT-1 through CT-8 define role checks and rejection envelopes, yet §15.1 and BaseAgent.guard() cross-references in CA-13-B-v3-Q1 never map these new gtm_bar_admin_override.v1 kinds into the EXECUTOR_REGISTRY or ScoreEvaluator.js decision matrix.
