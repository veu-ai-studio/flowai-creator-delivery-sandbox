**01. [Slot 1] Breaks §19 quorum thresholds**

> §19 Governance sets quorum ≥7/10 LIVE-OK and supermajority ≥8/10 ENGAGED. A 3-slot mini-Panel cannot satisfy these metrics, so any amendment approved this way would be procedurally invalid unless §19 is also amended, which this proposal does not address.

> Locked Rule 5 mandates Multi-AI peer review (10-AI Panel) and Locked Rule 17 requires ≥7/10 Panel review before any W0x→CEO action. Shrinking to 3 directly contradicts these binding constraints without proposing corresponding rule changes.

**03. [Slot 1] Underspecified low-risk triage**

> The proposal relies on the spec author flagging 'low risk', creating a conflict of interest and lacking an objective rubric. This contradicts the CA-n process in §18.2 (engagement thresholds) and offers no mechanism to audit or contest misclassification.

**04. [Slot 1] False-consensus rollback gap**

> Rollback only triggers on internal disagreement within the mini-Panel. If all three models agree incorrectly (false consensus), there is no escalation to the full 10-slot Panel, weakening the safety net relative to current practice.

**05. [Slot 1] Homogeneous model/regional bias risk**

> The fixed trio (gpt-5 US, claude-opus-4 US, deepseek-r1 Asia) concentrates vendor and regional biases, reducing cross-model diversity that the current 10-slot design implicitly leverages. This mirrors §8.1 concerns where Agent #11/#14 impose carve-outs for security/legal exposure; governance should not hard-code a narrow set without similar safeguards.

**06. [Slot 1] Weaker audit/compliance signal**

> §14 GovernanceAuditLog and §18.3 archive discipline rely on robust engagement evidence. Moving to 3 reviewers erodes the credibility of 'ENGAGED' signals promised in §19 and may undercut Clearance Protocol expectations for documented oversight.

**07. [Slot 2] Destroys geographic diversity mandate**

> The proposal replaces 10 geographically-distributed slots with 3 slots (2 US, 1 Asia), violating the SSOT's own democratization mission (§2) which explicitly targets Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America, and other underserved regions. This concentrates decision power in US/Asia tech centers.

**08. [Slot 2] Eliminates engagement-filter protection**

> §19 requires ≥7/10 ENGAGED votes for supermajority. With only 3 slots, a single TANGENTIAL/EVASIVE response could deadlock the mini-Panel entirely, forcing constant escalation to full Panel and negating any efficiency gains.

**09. [Slot 2] No audit trail for risk classification**

> The proposal lacks any GovernanceAuditLog integration (§14) for tracking who classified something as 'low risk' and why. This creates an unauditable backdoor where contentious changes could be rubber-stamped by gaming the risk classification.

**10. [Slot 2] Violates Locked Rule 5 explicitly**

> §25 Locked Rule 5 states 'Multi-AI peer review (10-AI Panel) mandatory for substantive outputs.' The proposal directly contradicts this by allowing 3-AI review for some outputs, with no amendment to the Locked Rule itself.

**11. [Slot 3] Lack of Transparency**

> The proposal does not provide clear information on how the mini-Panel will be selected and what criteria will be used to determine 'low risk' consultations. This lack of transparency could lead to biased decision-making and undermine the integrity of the Panel.

**12. [Slot 3] Insufficient Expertise**

> Reducing the Panel size from 10 to 3 members may lead to a lack of diverse expertise and perspectives, potentially resulting in poorly informed decisions. The proposal does not address how the mini-Panel will ensure that it has the necessary expertise to make informed decisions.

**13. [Slot 3] Inadequate Rollback Mechanism**

> The proposal's rollback mechanism, which convenes the full 10-slot Panel in case of disagreement, may not be sufficient to address potential errors or biases introduced by the mini-Panel. The proposal does not provide clear guidelines on how the full Panel will review and correct decisions made by the mini-Panel.

**14. [Slot 4] Inadequate Geographic Representation**

> The proposed mini-Panel lacks representation from Africa and Europe, which are key target markets according to §2. This could lead to biased decisions that do not account for the needs of these regions.

**15. [Slot 4] Risk of Inconsistent Decisions**

> Reducing the Panel to 3 slots increases the risk of inconsistent decisions due to the smaller sample size. This could lead to more frequent escalations to the full Panel, negating the proposed efficiency gains.

**16. [Slot 4] Lack of Redundancy in Decision Making**

> With only 3 members, the loss of any one member's input (due to technical issues or other reasons) would have a disproportionate impact on the decision-making process. The current 10-member Panel provides redundancy and robustness.

**17. [Slot 5] Lack of clarity on mini-panel composition**

> The proposal suggests replacing the current 10-slot Panel with a 3-slot mini-Panel, but it is unclear how the mini-Panel will be composed. Will it be a fixed set of three LLMs, or will it be dynamically selected based on some criteria? The proposal should provide more details on the composition of the mini-Panel.

**18. [Slot 5] Potential for bias in mini-panel composition**

> The proposal suggests using gpt-5 (US), claude-opus-4 (US), and deepseek-r1 (Asia) as the mini-Panel members. However, this composition may introduce bias, as it only includes LLMs from the US and Asia. The proposal should consider including LLMs from other regions to ensure a more diverse and representative mini-Panel.

**19. [Slot 5] Lack of clarity on the rollback path**

> The proposal mentions a rollback path in case the mini-Panel disagrees with itself, but it is unclear how this will be implemented. Will the full 10-slot Panel be convened automatically, or will there be a manual process to trigger the rollback? The proposal should provide more details on the rollback path.

**20. [Slot 6] Self-referential low-risk gating**

> §18.2 requires ≥7/10 ENGAGED votes for a supermajority. The proposal lets the spec author self-flag 'low_risk:true' in frontmatter to bypass this threshold entirely. This creates a conflict of interest: authors can mark any proposal low-risk to escape the supermajority gate. The CANONICAL_REFERENCE §18 has no provision for self-asserted risk classification; no independent validator is defined.

**21. [Slot 6] Geographic monoculture in mini-Panel**

> The proposed mini-Panel (gpt-5 US + claude-opus-4 US + deepseek-r1 Asia) has 2/3 US-based models vs no geographic constraint on the current 10-slot panel. §19 requires the Panel to serve products across Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America, and South/Southeast Asia per §2 democratization mission. A US-dominant mini-Panel systematically overlooks regional regulatory, cultural, and legal contexts (GDPR nuances, local accessibility laws, censorship regimes).

**22. [Slot 6] Reduced diversity degrades adversarial catch-rate**

> The Panel's canonical purpose (§19 + Locked Rule 5) is 'multi-AI peer review mandatory for substantive outputs.' A 3-slot panel reduces viewpoint diversity by ~70%. The adversarial value of 10-AI review is the orthogonal failure-mode coverage across 10 different model architectures and training distributions. A 3-slot panel is 3× more likely to share a blind spot, not 3× more likely to catch it — a Nash equilibrium failure in adversarial consensus.

**23. [Slot 6] Rollback trigger 'disagrees with itself' is undefined**

> The proposal specifies rollback to full Panel only 'if the mini-Panel disagrees with itself.' No threshold is given: does this mean unanimous disagreement (3-0), simple split (2-1), or any dissent (2-1 with one abstain)? §19 defines Panel supermajority as ≥8/10 ENGAGED; a 3-slot analog is unspecified. Without explicit thresholds, the rollback path is un-implementable — engineering cannot wire an undefined condition.

**24. [Slot 6] Flake amplification in small panels**

> The proposal claims 3 slots reduce 'envelope-flake retries.' However, envelope flaking (a single model's contradictory stances across identical contexts) is worse in small panels because: (a) a single flaky model constitutes 33% of a 3-slot vote vs 10% of a 10-slot vote; (b) the 10-slot panel's aggregation absorbs per-model flaking as noise; (c) a 3-slot split vote with one flaky model is statistically indistinguishable from a legitimate 2-1 split with no recourse. The proposal misattributes the flake problem to panel size rather than to model-level instability.

**25. [Slot 7] Vague risk classification**

> The proposal fails to define 'low risk' criteria (§15a), creating ambiguity. Misclassification could push critical decisions like security overrides (§13.1) or Orchestra auto-admission (§8.1) to a mini-Panel lacking diverse perspectives.

**26. [Slot 7] Regional bias in mini-Panel**

> Mini-Panel composition (gpt-5 US, deepseek-r1 Asia) violates Locked Rule 5's multi-region review principle. This risks overlooking locale-specific impacts on FlowAI's target underserved markets (§2), like rural accessibility or language nuances.

**27. [Slot 7] Ineffective rollback trigger**

> Rollback only on mini-Panel 'disagreement' lacks clear thresholds (§15a). Unanimous but incorrect low-risk approvals (e.g., flawed cost-governance changes to §7.6’s $15/run ceiling) wouldn’t trigger full review, causing silent failures.

**28. [Slot 7] Undermines engagement rigor**

> Reducing slots contradicts Locked Rule 17’s ≥7/10 engagement threshold for validity. Mini-Panel’s 3 slots cannot satisfy quorum, risking hasty consultations that bypass §18’s supermajority safeguards for amendments.

**29. [Slot 8] Low-risk is self-serving**

> The proposal makes the spec author the effective risk arbiter by relying on a label they control, rather than a separately governed rubric. In §SQ2 this creates a delegation loop where the same actor who benefits from faster passage can classify contentious changes as low risk. That is especially dangerous for governance changes because the label is not independently auditable.

**30. [Slot 8] Panel shrink weakens dissent**

> A 3-slot mini-Panel cannot approximate the diversity of failure detection in the canonical 10-slot process described in the reference. The proposal removes most of the adversarial surface area for catching hidden assumptions, cross-domain regressions, and consensus hallucinations. For panel-governance itself, that is a particularly bad trade because the change alters the guardrail used to evaluate future guardrails.

**31. [Slot 8] Rollback is too late**

> The rollback path only triggers if the mini-Panel disagrees with itself, which does not cover the more likely failure mode: all three slots agree on a bad decision. §SQ1 therefore permits false confidence, especially on low-risk-but-high-blast-radius amendments that look trivial in isolation. Once a governance change is recorded, later correction does not erase the operational and social cost of the bad decision.

**32. [Slot 8] Cost savings are overstated**

> The proposal cites a 70% cost reduction, but it ignores the cost of false negatives, rework, and the downstream burden of undoing bad approvals. For governance consultations, the dominant cost is often not inference spend but the cost of an incorrect ratification. A cheaper panel that increases mistaken approvals can easily be more expensive end-to-end.

**33. [Slot 9] Lack of clear risk assessment**

> The proposal does not provide a clear method for determining what constitutes 'low risk' (§SQ2 options vary widely). Without a standardized risk assessment, the decision to shrink the Panel may be arbitrary.

**34. [Slot 9] Potential for biased mini-Panel**

> The proposed mini-Panel composition (gpt-5, claude-opus-4, deepseek-r1) may introduce bias due to the specific models chosen. Different models may have varying strengths and weaknesses that could impact the review outcome.

**35. [Slot 9] Rollback path ambiguity**

> The proposal mentions a rollback path if the mini-Panel disagrees with itself, but it is unclear how this disagreement would be measured and what the exact procedure for convening the full Panel would be.

**36. [Slot 10] Mini-Panel composition bias**

> §15a specifies gpt-5 (US), claude-opus-4 (US), deepseek-r1 (Asia) with no mechanism to rotate or audit model drift; this directly violates the 10-slot diversity requirement in §19 Panel quorum rules and §25 Locked Rule 5.

**37. [Slot 10] Low-risk triage undefined**

> The proposal delegates 'low risk' classification to the spec author without referencing the fixed rubric in §19 or the engagement filter in PANEL_INFRASTRUCTURE.md §6, creating an un-auditable single point of failure before any mini-Panel even convenes.

**38. [Slot 10] Rollback path insufficient**
