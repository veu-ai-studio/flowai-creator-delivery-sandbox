## All distinct objections (32)
**01. [Slot 1] Unbounded recommendation generation risk**

> §7 item #8's minimal envelope lacks any rate limits or caps on recommendation volume. Without PA-1..PA-N invariants, agents could flood operators with thousands of low-quality recommendations per run, creating noise that obscures critical findings.

**02. [Slot 1] Cost estimation without validation**

> The new cost.effort_hours_estimate field in §7 has no validation bounds or accuracy requirements. Agents could emit wildly incorrect estimates (0.1 hours for major refactors, 1000 hours for trivial changes) with no conformance test to catch this.

**03. [Slot 1] Rollback procedure underspecified**

> §7's rollback.revert_procedure is a free-text string with no schema or required steps. CA16A-v2-CT-3 only warns on non-revertable accepted recs but doesn't validate that revert_procedure is actually executable or complete.

**04. [Slot 1] Defer-window config lacks audit trail**

> §7 item #8 + §3 admin config allows per-product defer windows [1,365] days but has no requirement to log changes to recommendation_defer_window_days. An admin could set 365-day deferrals to effectively suppress recommendations without trace.

**05. [Slot 3] Lack of Rollback**

> The proposal does not provide a clear rollback mechanism in case the Redesign/Build Environment causes unintended changes to the ProductSSOT. This is a significant risk, as it could lead to data loss or corruption. The §29 Redesign/Build Environment section should include a detailed rollback plan to mitigate this risk.

**06. [Slot 3] Insufficient Validation**

> The proposal does not provide sufficient validation rules for the proactive recommendations envelope. The minimal canonical envelope is too permissive, and the full schema deferred to engineering dispatch may not be thoroughly tested. This could lead to invalid or inconsistent data being stored in the ProductSSOT.

**07. [Slot 3] Overly Complex Taxonomy**

> The 6-class Multi-Format taxonomy may be overly complex and difficult to maintain. The proposal should consider a simpler taxonomy or provide a clear justification for the need for six classes. Additionally, the proposal should include a plan for handling new or emerging formats that do not fit into the existing taxonomy.

**08. [Slot 4] Overly Complex Deferral**

> The proposal defers too many critical details to engineering dispatch, such as the full schema invariants (PA-1..PA-N). This deferral could lead to inconsistencies and lack of clarity in the implementation phase. Specifically, §7 item #8 defers the full schema to engineering, which may cause misunderstandings and misalignments.

**09. [Slot 4] Lack of Rollback Mechanism**

> The proposal introduces a `rollback` object but does not specify a concrete rollback mechanism for accepted recommendations. This could lead to situations where accepted recommendations cannot be safely reverted, causing potential disruptions. The `rollback` object in §7 item #8 should include a mandatory revert procedure.

**10. [Slot 4] Vague Admin Gating**

> The wording for the Redesign/Build Environment (RB-Env) is vague regarding admin gating. It states that RB-Env is admin-gated but does not specify the exact workflow or approval process. This vagueness could lead to misinterpretations and potential security risks. The scope statement in the Redesign/Build Environment section should be more explicit.

**11. [Slot 5] Minimal Envelope Risk**

> The minimal canonical envelope may not capture all necessary details for effective implementation, leading to potential gaps in the proactive recommendations system.

**12. [Slot 5] Defer-Window Complexity**

> The per-product defer-window configuration adds complexity without clear benefits, potentially leading to inconsistent application of recommendations across products.

**13. [Slot 5] Section Number Ambiguity**

> Deferring the section number for the Redesign/Build Environment creates ambiguity and may lead to confusion in the implementation and maintenance of the feature.

**14. [Slot 6] Cost object underspecified**

> The `cost` object in the `proactive_recommendation` schema (§7) lacks concrete guidance on how `effort_hours_estimate`, `risk_tier`, and `blast_radius` should be determined, leading to inconsistent application and potential gaming of the system. The lack of clear definitions for `risk_tier` and `blast_radius` makes them subjective and open to interpretation.

**15. [Slot 6] Rollback procedure ambiguity**

> The `rollback` object's `revert_procedure` field (§7) is optional and human-readable, which is insufficient for ensuring reliable reversibility.  Without a standardized, machine-executable rollback mechanism, the system relies on potentially error-prone manual processes, increasing the risk of failed rollbacks and system instability. The 'revertable' boolean alone is insufficient.

**16. [Slot 6] Deferral window clamping risk**

> The per-product defer-window config (§7) clamps out-of-bounds values, but the notification via `agent.product_registry.defer_window_clamped.v1` might be missed by admins, leading to unexpected behavior.  A more prominent warning or blocking mechanism might be necessary to ensure admins are aware of the clamping.

**17. [Slot 6] Ambiguity detection threshold**

> The stricter classifier in CA-16-C-Q2, with a confidence floor of 0.85, might lead to excessive `target_class_ambiguous` findings, overwhelming operators with dispute resolution tasks. The choice of 0.85 seems arbitrary and lacks empirical justification. This could lead to alert fatigue.

**18. [Slot 7] Deferred schema risks divergence**

> CA-16-A v2's deferred full schema to engineering dispatch creates future compatibility risks. Without canonical PA-1..PA-N invariants in §7, engineering implementations may diverge from intent, particularly around mandatory validation rules. This violates SSOT's single-source-of-truth principle and could break conformance tests like CA16A-v2-CT-2.

**19. [Slot 7] Rollback procedure inadequacy**

> The new rollback.revert_procedure field (§7 Proactive Recommendations) lacks enforcement mechanisms. Conformance test CA16A-v2-CT-3 only flags non-revertable recs without blocking acceptance. In complex deployments, manual revert steps may be insufficient for stateful changes, risking system instability when reversing implemented recommendations.

**20. [Slot 7] Ambiguity severity inflation**

> CA-16-C v2's stricter classifier (§6 Multi-Format) mandates 'high' severity for target_class_ambiguous at <0.85 confidence. This could flood operators with false-positive high-severity tickets for legitimate hybrid formats like PWAs, diluting attention from genuine critical findings and violating §7.6's severity calibration principles.

**21. [Slot 7] Defer-window governance gap**

> Per-product defer-window (CA-16-A-Q4) allows operators to set 365-day deferrals without admin oversight. This enables indefinite suppression of recommendations via recurrent deferrals, contravening the proactive intent. CA16A-v2-CT-5/6 only validate clamping, not governance of excessive deferral patterns.

**22. [Slot 8] Mandatory fields still undercut optionality**

> In CA-16-A v2, `purpose_record_link` and `market_definition_link` are made optional, but the envelope still requires `category`, `title`, `rationale`, and `source` without defining how null/empty recommendations are represented when the system has only a weak heuristic suggestion. That creates a gap between the claimed “minimal canonical envelope” and real emission paths from §7.6 / §10.1, especially for cross-cutting suggestions that do not map cleanly to a single product or run.

**23. [Slot 8] Rollback is advisory only**

> The new `rollback` object adds `revertable` and `revert_procedure`, but CA16A-v2-CT-3 explicitly says non-revertable accepted recommendations are only “flagged but not blocked.” This preserves the exact failure mode the Panel objected to in v1: operators can still accept recommendations that cannot be safely undone, with no hard gate or enforcement path in the canonical contract.

**24. [Slot 8] Defer-window can hide urgent recs**

> CA-16-A-Q4 moves defer timing into a per-product knob with bounds [1,365], but the spec does not define any escalation or override behavior for high-severity recommendations that are deferred long-term. A product owner could set 365 days and effectively suppress repeated resurfacing of a critical systemic recommendation, which is a concrete governance regression compared with a uniform shorter window.

**25. [Slot 8] RB-Env numbering deferral is incomplete**

> CA-16-B-Q1 defers the section number for Redesign/Build Environment, yet the same block continues to assert canonical scope, atomic-audit-write, and mandatory cross-references to CA-14 fix-safety invariants. This creates a half-canonical surface: implementers must wire behavior now, but the spec avoids the one piece that anchors documentation and review navigation, increasing divergence risk across mirrored copies and later amendments.

**26. [Slot 8] Silent fallback removed without evidence**

> CA-16-C-Q2 tightens classification by emitting `target_class_ambiguous.v1` whenever confidence <0.85, but the proposal does not specify calibration data, class priors, or how to handle known ambiguous hybrids like PWA/SaaS shells beyond operator dispute resolution. That can convert previously working generic fallbacks into a high-severity alert storm, increasing manual workload without proving better classification accuracy.

**27. [Slot 9] Unclear Impact of Cost Object**

> The new 'cost' object in proactive recommendations (§7 item #8) includes 'effort_hours_estimate', 'risk_tier', and 'blast_radius'. However, it's unclear how these fields will be used in practice and how they will affect the decision-making process for operators.

**28. [Slot 9] Rollback Mechanism Ambiguity**

> The 'rollback' object is introduced with a 'revertable' boolean and an optional 'revert_procedure'. The proposal lacks clarity on how this will be enforced or validated, particularly for complex recommendations.

**29. [Slot 9] Defer-Window Config Flexibility**

> The per-product defer-window configuration (§7 item #8 + §3 admin config) allows for a wide range of values ([1,365] days). This flexibility might lead to inconsistent practices across different products and operators.

**30. [Slot 10] Minimal envelope omits validation**

> §7 item #8 defines cost.risk_tier and rollback.revertable but states full schema invariants (PA-1..PA-N) are deferred to engineering dispatch; no canonical rule enforces atomicity with ProductSSOT delta_log writes per §7.5.

**31. [Slot 10] Stricter classifier lacks workflow**

> CA-16-C-v2-Q2 requires target_class_ambiguous at high severity for confidence <0.85 yet provides no operator dispute resolution path or tie-in to §6 deliberate error-state triggers or §11 ClearanceRecord steps.

**32. [Slot 10] Per-product defer desync risk**

> CA-16-A-Q4 adds recommendation_defer_window_days to product_registry without mandating atomic update alongside proactive_recommendation.disposition in the same run, violating the atomic guarantee claimed in §7.5.
