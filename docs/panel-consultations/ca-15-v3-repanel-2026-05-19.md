## All distinct objections (34)
**01. [Slot 1] Drift detection without purpose field**

> §28-bis.1 PURPOSE_DRIFT detection relies on 'operator-maintained purpose-notes file' outside ProductSSOT, but CA-15-B v3 removes all purpose information from canonical state. This creates an undefined reference - drift detection cannot function without a canonical purpose baseline to compare against.

**02. [Slot 1] Governance directory scope gameable**

> CA-15-D-Q2's `src/lib/governance/**` scope for CEO re-sign is trivially circumvented by moving critical governance logic to adjacent directories like `src/lib/scoring/` or `src/lib/clearance/`. The directory-based trigger creates a false security boundary that sophisticated actors can route around.

**03. [Slot 1] Phase B pass underspecified**

> §28-bis exit condition #5 requires 'Phase B pass per CA-14-A canonical + Agent #21 Phase B charter' but the canonical reference excerpt provides no definition of Phase B pass criteria. This creates an undefined exit gate that could block loop termination indefinitely.

**04. [Slot 1] Tooling-only enforcement toothless**

> §10.1 moves content_quality + accessibility to `scripts/lint-*.mjs` tooling that 'emit as PR-warnings only' without participating in §7.6 score or Quality Audit. This relegates critical quality dimensions to ignorable warnings, allowing degraded outputs to achieve GTM clearance.

**05. [Slot 2] No LLM purpose check**

> In §3, the proposal removes the LLM-judged purpose-alignment check from the Purpose-Driven Optimization loop exit conditions. This could lead to situations where the product's purpose is not adequately aligned with its development, potentially resulting in misaligned outputs.

**06. [Slot 2] Hard blocking gate inflexibility**

> In §4, reverting to a hard blocking gate for §27 may introduce rigidity, preventing necessary flexibility in handling conformance issues. This could delay product releases unnecessarily if minor issues are treated with the same severity as major ones.

**07. [Slot 2] Purpose information exclusion**

> In §2, the complete exclusion of purpose information from ProductSSOT could lead to a lack of structured documentation of product intent, making it harder for operators to maintain a clear understanding of the product's goals and evolution.

**08. [Slot 3] Lack of Rollback**

> The proposal does not provide a clear rollback strategy in case the changes introduced by CA-15-C v3 cause unintended consequences. This is a concern because the changes affect the Purpose-Driven Optimization loop and the §7.6 score calculation, which are critical components of the system. Specifically, §28-bis does not outline a rollback plan in case the numeric-floor-only exit conditions cause the loop to exit prematurely.

**09. [Slot 3] Insufficient Threshold Justification**

> The proposal introduces heuristic-bootstrap thresholds for PURPOSE_DRIFT detection (20% and 50% token-delta) without providing sufficient empirical justification or explanation for these specific values. This lack of justification raises concerns about the effectiveness and potential impact of these thresholds on the system. §28-bis.1 does not provide a clear rationale for these thresholds, which could lead to unintended consequences.

**10. [Slot 3] Potential for Operator Confusion**

> The removal of the `product_purpose` field from ProductSSOT and the introduction of operator-maintained purpose notes could lead to operator confusion and inconsistencies in purpose tracking. This is because operators may not understand the implications of maintaining purpose notes outside of ProductSSOT, and the system does not provide clear guidance on how to handle purpose changes. §15-B v3 does not address this potential issue, which could lead to errors and inconsistencies in the system.

**11. [Slot 4] Complexity in Self-Renewal Executor**

> The Self-Renewal Executor's diff-only rule in §6 might create difficulties in maintaining code quality. Large rewrites are forbidden, which could limit the ability to address complex issues effectively.

**12. [Slot 4] Ambiguity in Purpose Drift**

> The tiered PURPOSE_DRIFT annotation in §28-bis.1 might lead to ambiguity in handling drift. The heuristic-bootstrap thresholds could be inaccurate, leading to incorrect drift classifications.

**13. [Slot 4] Overlap in Quality Audit Dimensions**

> The UI/UX dimension in §10.1 overlaps with the Content Quality checks, leading to potential double-reporting and confusion in the quality audit process.

**14. [Slot 5] Tooling-only checks**

> Relocating content quality and accessibility checks to tooling may lead to these checks being overlooked or ignored by operators, reducing overall system quality.

**15. [Slot 5] No purpose in SSOT**

> Removing purpose information from ProductSSOT may lead to a lack of clarity and direction for the product, making it harder to align development efforts with business goals.

**16. [Slot 5] Hard blocking gate**

> Reverting §27 to a hard blocking gate may cause delays in the clearance process, as operators will be required to resolve all conformance issues before proceeding.

**17. [Slot 6] Drift threshold fragility**

> The 20%/50% token-delta thresholds for PURPOSE_DRIFT are heuristic and lack empirical justification (§28-bis.1). This could lead to false positives or negatives, undermining the drift detection mechanism and potentially causing unnecessary alerts or missed critical deviations.

**18. [Slot 6] Hard blocking gate override**

> Even with the §27 revert to a hard blocking gate, the admin override per §19 still exists (CA-15-D-Q1). This undermines the purpose of the hard blocking gate, as an admin can bypass conformance failures, potentially leading to the deployment of non-conforming code.

**19. [Slot 6] Limited CEO re-sign scope**

> The CEO re-sign trigger scope is limited to `src/lib/governance/**` (CA-15-D-Q2). This may not be sufficient to capture all relevant changes that could impact governance, as other directories or files could also contain code that affects governance policies or processes.

**20. [Slot 6] Lack of LLM purpose check**

> The proposal removes LLM-judged purpose-alignment checks from the loop exit (CA-15-C-Q1). While the rationale is to avoid shadow metrics, this could lead to the system optimizing for numeric scores without ensuring that the product actually fulfills its intended purpose.

**21. [Slot 7] Arbitrary drift thresholds**

> §28-bis.1's token-change drift tiers (20%/50%) lack empirical justification. These thresholds were admitted as heuristic-bootstrap in the changelog table yet will trigger critical production freezes. No validation exists for token-delta accurately reflecting purpose drift severity.

**22. [Slot 7] Governance directory over-scoping**

> CA-15-D-v3-Q2 triggers CEO re-sign for ANY change in entire `src/lib/governance/` directory. This includes non-substantive files like comments or configs, creating operational friction. The broad scope contradicts the locked rule of CEO paste-only automation.

**23. [Slot 7] Purpose traceability gap**

> Removing purpose from ProductSSOT (§2) eliminates canonical purpose tracking while retaining PURPOSE_DRIFT checks. Drift detection now relies on external operator notes with no validation mechanism, creating inconsistency between the removed field and retained drift semantics.

**24. [Slot 7] Hard gate override vulnerability**

> §27 revert to hard blocking gate (D-Q1) retains §19 admin override path. This creates a loophole where operators can bypass conformance checks under pressure, undermining the very enforcement the revert aims to strengthen.

**25. [Slot 8] §28-bis is misnamed**

> The proposed loop keeps the label “Purpose-Driven Optimization” while explicitly removing any purpose-alignment check from §28-bis. That creates a semantic mismatch between the mechanism and its title, which will confuse operators and future maintainers about what the loop is actually optimizing.

**26. [Slot 8] Tiered drift is untethered**

> §28-bis.1 uses shingle-based token-delta heuristics on operator-maintained notes outside ProductSSOT, but gives no normalization rules for copied text, formatting changes, or multi-document notes. The 20%/50% thresholds are admitted as heuristic-bootstrap, so the same content could oscillate between minor and major drift without a stable canonical interpretation.

**27. [Slot 8] Hard block plus override conflict**

> §27 is reverted to a hard blocking gate, but the drafted text also preserves an admin override path via §19-equivalent override language in CA-15-D-Q1. That combination is internally inconsistent: if conformance failure blocks clearance, an override becomes a hidden bypass unless the scope and audit conditions are precisely bounded.

**28. [Slot 8] External purpose notes are brittle**

> Removing purpose information from ProductSSOT and pushing it into operator notes/README files makes the core governance state non-atomic and non-auditable. The draft then depends on those external notes for drift detection, so a missing, stale, or renamed note can silently disable a central governance signal without any canonical record of the failure.

**29. [Slot 9] Unclear §7.6 score impact**

> The proposal removes `product_purpose` field but retains §7.6 score calculation. It's unclear how this score will be affected without purpose information (§2).

**30. [Slot 9] Drift detection risks**

> The tiered PURPOSE_DRIFT annotation (§28-bis.1) uses heuristic thresholds (20%/50%). These may not be effective in practice, potentially leading to incorrect drift classification.

**31. [Slot 9] §27 revert implications**

> Reverting §27 to a hard blocking gate (§4) may cause operational delays if conformance issues arise frequently. The impact on operator workflow is not clearly assessed.

**32. [Slot 10] §27 hard blocking gate deadlock risk**

> CA-15-D v3 reverts §27 to HARD BLOCKING GATE over entire src/lib/governance/ directory; a single medium-severity conformance entry now blocks §11 Step 6 clearance with no automatic bypass except §19 95/95 override, creating potential infinite loop when operator notes drift is detected outside ProductSSOT.

**33. [Slot 10] PURPOSE_DRIFT heuristic thresholds**

> §28-bis.1 defines 20%/50% token-delta tiers on operator-notes files that CA-15-B v3 explicitly places outside canonical SSOT; no validation mechanism exists for shingle-based diff accuracy and thresholds are labeled heuristic-bootstrap without telemetry accumulation path.

**34. [Slot 10] Tooling lints decoupled from scoring**

> CA-15-A v3 moves content_quality + accessibility to scripts/lint-*.mjs emitting only PR warnings; this decouples them from §7.6 formula and §10.1 5-dimension Self-Audit, yet §10.1 still claims 95/95 governance threshold without updated integration spec.
