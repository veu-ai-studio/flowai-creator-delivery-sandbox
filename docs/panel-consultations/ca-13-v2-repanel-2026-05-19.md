## All distinct objections (34)
**01. [Slot 1] Operator override creates governance bypass**

> The `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob in CA-13-A v2 allows operators to set GTM bar as low as 75, undermining the CEO directive for ≥95. This creates a permanent escape hatch where operators can ship at 75 indefinitely by never setting the override, making the '≥95 after first product' promise unenforceable.

**02. [Slot 1] Capability-keyed naming loses agent traceability**

> CA-13-B v2's switch to `crawl-write-executor` and `orchestra-admission-executor` obscures which primary agent owns the sibling. When debugging production issues, operators won't know that `crawl-write-executor` belongs to Agent #21 without cross-referencing the EXECUTOR_REGISTRY, increasing operational complexity.

**03. [Slot 1] Phase B prerequisite enforcement unclear**

> §11 Step 5 references 'Phase B pass per CA-14-A canonical' but CA-13-A v2 eliminates the Near-GTM conditional band without clarifying how partial Phase B passes (some paths skipped) affect clearance. The interaction between hard thresholds and Phase B skip-disclosure creates ambiguity about what constitutes a 'pass'.

**04. [Slot 1] No migration path for in-flight products**

> Products currently in the pipeline targeting 75-89 Demo-ready have no documented migration path if their operator later sets `gtm_ready_bar_override: 95`. The proposal doesn't specify whether the override applies retroactively to already-cleared products or only to new clearances, creating versioning confusion.

**05. [Slot 2] Operator-config knob risks inconsistency**

> The introduction of the `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob in §7.6 could lead to inconsistencies across products if operators set different thresholds. This could undermine the uniformity of the GTM readiness criteria and complicate governance.

**06. [Slot 2] Elimination of Near-GTM band lacks flexibility**

> By eliminating the 90–94 Near-GTM conditional band, the proposal removes flexibility for products that are close to being market-ready but not quite there. This could delay the launch of products that are otherwise viable with minor adjustments.

**07. [Slot 2] Sibling naming convention may cause confusion**

> Switching to capability-keyed sibling charter naming could cause confusion if the capabilities are not clearly defined or if they overlap with other agents. This might lead to misunderstandings about the roles and responsibilities of each agent.

**08. [Slot 3] Lack of Rollback**

> The proposal does not provide a clear rollback strategy in case the implementation of the `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob causes unintended consequences. This could lead to difficulties in reverting changes and potentially causing harm to the system.

**09. [Slot 3] Insufficient Testing**

> The proposal does not outline a comprehensive testing plan to ensure the correctness and safety of the `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob. This could lead to errors or security vulnerabilities being introduced into the system.

**10. [Slot 3] Inconsistent Authorization**

> The proposal introduces a new authorization mechanism for the `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob, but it is unclear how this mechanism will interact with existing authorization systems. This could lead to inconsistencies or security vulnerabilities in the authorization process.

**11. [Slot 4] Operator override complexity**

> The introduction of `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` adds complexity to the system. Operators may set arbitrary values without understanding the full implications, leading to inconsistent product quality. The §7.6 score bands should remain canonical and uniform across products to ensure consistency.

**12. [Slot 4] Lack of migration path**

> The proposal does not provide a clear migration path for existing products that are already in the pipeline. This could lead to inconsistencies and potential failures in the clearance process for products that were initiated under the previous guidelines.

**13. [Slot 4] Potential for misuse**

> The operator-configurable knob could be misused by operators to bypass the strict quality standards set by the canonical §7.6 score bands. This could lead to products being cleared for GTM that do not meet the necessary quality standards, undermining the overall integrity of the system.

**14. [Slot 5] Operator Override Risk**

> The introduction of the `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob allows operators to set a higher GTM readiness bar, but this could lead to inconsistent application of the GTM readiness criteria across different products, potentially undermining the integrity of the GTM process.

**15. [Slot 5] Complexity in Governance**

> The proposal adds another layer of complexity to the governance model by introducing an operator-configurable GTM bar, which may lead to confusion and difficulties in maintaining consistent governance practices across the organization.

**16. [Slot 5] Potential for Abuse**

> The capability-keyed naming for sibling charters could be exploited by operators to bypass certain governance checks, especially if the naming conventions are not strictly enforced or monitored.

**17. [Slot 6] Operator Config Knob Risk**

> The `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob in §7.6 introduces a risk of inconsistent GTM readiness standards across products, potentially leading to customer confusion and diluted brand perception if some products are launched with lower quality scores than others. This undermines the purpose of having a standardized GTM readiness process.

**18. [Slot 6] Clamping Semantics**

> The silent clamping of out-of-bounds `gtm_ready_bar_override` values to [75, 100] in §7.6, while notifying admins, could lead to unexpected behavior and debugging challenges. Operators might assume their configured value is being used, leading to incorrect assumptions about GTM readiness.

**19. [Slot 6] Reconciliation Paragraph Redundancy**

> The §19.0 reconciliation paragraph distinguishing Self-Audit 95/95 from GTM Readiness bar seems redundant, as the §10 governance-mechanisms table already covers these distinctions. This adds unnecessary complexity to the document.

**20. [Slot 6] Sibling Naming Convention**

> While capability-keyed naming for sibling charters is generally better, the specific name `orchestra-admission-executor` might be too specific. It focuses on the 'admission' aspect, potentially overlooking other responsibilities the executor might have in the future.

**21. [Slot 7] §7.6 override risk**

> The operator-configurable GTM bar (gtm_ready_bar_override) lacks enforcement for CEO directives. §7.6 allows operators to set thresholds below the CEO-mandated 95 without consequences, creating misalignment between executive intent and implementation. The clamping mechanism only handles numerical bounds, not policy compliance.

**22. [Slot 7] Sibling naming ambiguity**

> Capability-keyed naming for executors (crawl-write-executor/orchestra-admission-executor) in §15.1 creates ambiguity. Unlike agent-specific names, these don't indicate primary association, risking confusion during incident debugging. The proposal lacks cross-referencing standards between primary agents and siblings.

**23. [Slot 7] HARD threshold fallout**

> Eliminating Near-GTM conditional band ignores operational realities. Products scoring 89 (Demo-ready) but blocked from conditional clearance due to a single high finding may undergo unnecessary rebuilds. §11 Step 5 HARD fails to account for context-dependent risk tolerance.

**24. [Slot 7] Phase B verification gap**

> CA-13-A-CT-4 conformance test only checks for conditional-clearance keywords but doesn't validate Phase B testing requirements. Agent #21's adversarial testing mandate (ENTRY 015) could be bypassed if implementation focuses solely on score thresholds without verifying interactive flow coverage.

**25. [Slot 8] Override weakens global defaults**

> In CA-13-A v2 §7.6, `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` can raise the GTM bar per product while leaving the canonical threshold at 75. That creates a split-brain policy where the same clearance step means different things across products, environments, and operator views, increasing the chance of misconfigured launches and ambiguous audit outcomes.

**26. [Slot 8] Silent clamp hides operator errors**

> The proposed `[75, 100]` clamping in CA-13-A v2 silently converts out-of-bounds values instead of rejecting them. In a governance path, silent coercion is dangerous because a mistaken `50` becomes a seemingly valid `75`, which can mask operational mistakes and make post-incident forensics harder than a hard failure would.

**27. [Slot 8] Hard gate removes recovery path**

> CA-13-A v2 eliminates the 90–94 Near-GTM conditional path from §11 Step 5 entirely. That means a product that is otherwise launchable but temporarily blocked by a narrow, fixable issue cannot use a supervised conditional path, which may increase time-to-market and encourage risky workarounds outside the formal clearance flow.

**28. [Slot 8] Sibling naming still underspecified**

> CA-13-B v2 switches to capability-keyed names like `crawl-write-executor` and `orchestra-admission-executor`, but the draft excerpt truncates the EXECUTOR_REGISTRY definitions and does not fully specify collision handling, lifecycle, or migration from the old keys. Without explicit compatibility rules, this risks orphaned permissions or duplicate executor entries during rollout.

**29. [Slot 9] Config knob lacks audit trail**

> The `product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override` knob is operator-configurable but lacks a clear audit trail. §7.6 mentions admin notification on out-of-bounds override, but not regular logging of changes.

**30. [Slot 9] Fallback clause ambiguity**

> The fallback clause for re-introducing a conditional path (§11 Step 5) requires ALL `high` findings to be Resolved. However, 'Resolved' is not clearly defined in the context of Self-Renewal cycles and post-fix score reflection.

**31. [Slot 9] Sibling charter naming inconsistency**

> CA-13-B-v2 switches to capability-keyed naming for sibling charters. However, the rationale does not clearly justify why this is superior to the previous agent-short-name-keyed approach, potentially introducing naming inconsistency.

**32. [Slot 10] gtm_ready_bar_override lacks migration**

> CA-13-A v2 §7.6 operator-config knob section defines product_registry.gtm_ready_bar_override with bounds [75,100] but names no migration path or existing-product backfill; §11 Step 5 row update references the knob without specifying how ClearanceRecord entities created pre-v2 are reconciled.

**33. [Slot 10] fallback clause weakens hard threshold**

> CA-13-A v2 Near-GTM elimination section's fallback clause still permits conditional paths if Panel re-introduces them, requiring only 'Resolved' high findings; this directly conflicts with §7.6 safety-invariants (a-e) that mandate diff-only + post-deploy regression guard without defining enforcement for the fallback.

**34. [Slot 10] EXECUTOR_REGISTRY sibling keys incomplete**

> CA-13-B v2 §15.1 rows 21/26 switch to capability-keyed names (crawl-write-executor, orchestra-admission-executor) but the EXECUTOR_REGISTRY typescript snippet omits required updates to BaseAgent.guard() authorityNeeded set membership and api/agent/21/execute.js wiring already referenced in §15.1.
